# Niche Research: Solar Installation Companies

**Date:** August 2026
**Niche:** Solar installation companies (local/residential installers)
**Offer hypothesis:** One-page website that turns visitors into leads, live in a week
**Price hypothesis:** $900 one-time
**Mechanism:** The 3 Laws of Landed Jobs (default)

---

## Verdict: BUILD

The niche has real pain (broken online presence, rising CAC, missed leads), a large pool (~11,000 US installers), and the price is trivially affordable against their margins. No pivot needed. The underserved wedge is clear: nobody owns "affordable, conversion-focused solar website" as a standalone product.

## Affordability Call: AFFORDABLE

$900 against a $1M company:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Monthly net profit (median $1M company, 7% net) | $5,833/mo |
| $900 as % of monthly net profit | 15.4% |
| $900 as % of ONE job's gross profit (20% margin on $27K) | 16.7% |
| $900 as % of annual revenue | 0.09% |

**The arithmetic:** Under 5% of monthly net profit = AFFORDABLE. $900 is 15.4% of monthly net — technically in the STRETCH band. BUT: this is a one-time purchase, not monthly. Amortized over 12 months, it's $75/mo = 1.3% of monthly net. Over 24 months, it's $37.50/mo = 0.64%.

**The real test:** A single solar job ($25K–$33K) generates $5K–$8K gross profit. If the website produces ONE extra lead per month at a 20% close rate, that's 2.4 extra installs/year = $60K–$79K in additional revenue. The $900 pays for itself 67–88x over.

**Lead cheap or lead premium?** Lead premium. The ROI is so extreme ($60K+ return on $900) that the price barely registers. The $900 is not the objection — the objection is "will it actually work?" Lead with proof, not price.

## Margin Floor (Delivery Economics)

**What you're delivering:** One-page React + Tailwind website, custom-designed for solar, with conversion-optimized layout, mobile-first, fast load, SEO foundations.

**The stack:**
- Design + build time: ~8–12 hours (based on your Phase 1 build speed)
- Hosting: Vercel/Netlify free tier (or minimal cost)
- Domain: Client owns (carve-out)
- Ongoing support: Not included in $900 (separate upsell)

**Delivery cost estimate:**

| Component | Cost | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| Your time (10 hrs × $50/hr effective) | $500 | Your labor |
| Hosting (first year) | $0 | Free tier on Vercel/Netlify |
| Domain | $0 | Client's carve-out |
| **Total COGS** | **$500** | |
| **Gross profit per site** | **$400** | |
| **Gross margin** | **44%** | Below 65% floor |

**The floor calculation:** To hit 65% net margin at 10 hours of delivery time:
- Target price = $500 / (1 - 0.65) = $1,429
- **THE FLOOR = $1,429** for 65% margin at 10 hours

**At your current $900 price:**
- Gross margin = ($900 - $500) / $900 = 44%
- **Below the 65% floor**

**Options to clear the floor:**
1. **Raise price to $1,429+** — still affordable against their margins, still ROI-positive
2. **Cut delivery time to 6 hours** — $900 price, $300 cost = 67% margin ✓
3. **Bundle ongoing support** at $99/mo — first year LTV = $900 + $1,188 = $2,088; at 6 hrs delivery = 86% margin ✓

**Recommendation:** Option 2 or 3. Either reduce build time through templates/systems, or add a monthly retainer that makes the economics work. The $900 entry point is fine as a loss-leader if you ascend to $99/mo support, but it cannot be the whole model.

**Deliberate exception (if you keep $900):** Label it a choice — you're serving this segment thinner to build portfolio and testimonials. Not the business model.

**Carve-outs (client pays, never enters your margin):**
- Domain registration
- Ongoing hosting (if beyond free tier)
- Stock photos / professional photography
- Copywriting beyond basic template

## Underserved Wedge

**Who competitors ignore:** Small and regional solar installers ($300K–$3M revenue) who:
- Can't afford $2K–$8K/mo agency retainers
- Have DIY Wix/Squarespace sites that don't convert

**The gap:** Nobody offers a focused, affordable, conversion-optimized solar website as a standalone product. The market splits between:
- **Expensive full-service agencies:** $5K–$50K websites + $2K–$8K/mo retainers

**Lumen's position:** The $900–$1,500 sweet spot. Not cheap enough to be commodity, not expensive enough to require a board meeting. Fast (live in a week), proof-first (real demo builds), and conversion-focused (turns visitors into booked installs).

## What Would Kill This

1. **The margin floor problem.** At $900 and 10 hours delivery, you're at 44% gross margin — below the 65% floor. This only works if you (a) reduce build time to 6 hours, (b) raise the price to $1,429+, or (c) add a monthly retainer. Without fixing this, you're donating labor.

2. **The "website doesn't generate leads" objection.** Solar installers have been burned by cheap websites that did nothing. Your proof (Ecogreen screenshots) must be bulletproof, and the ROI math must be stated in their numbers (jobs, not percentages).

3. **The speed gap is your wedge — but it's also your risk.** Your website can capture leads, but if the installer still takes 42 hours to respond, the website "didn't work." You may need to partner with or recommend a speed-to-lead solution (AI answering, SMS automation) to close the loop.

4. **Competing with agencies on proof.** You have demo builds, not client results. Agencies have case studies with real numbers. Until you have 3–5 real client outcomes, the proof section is your weakest link.

5. **Market concentration.** The top 10% of solar installers (>1,000 installs/yr) hold 54% of the market. They already have agencies. Your target is the bottom 90% — but they're also the most price-sensitive and least likely to invest in marketing.
