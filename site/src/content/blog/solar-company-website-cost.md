A professional website for a solar installation company costs between $3,000 and $25,000 in 2026. Template-based sites run $2,000 to $8,000, custom-designed sites with lead generation features cost $8,000 to $25,000, and enterprise solutions with advanced integrations start at $25,000. ## What drives the cost of a solar company website?

The price depends on three factors: customization level, features included, and build timeline.

**Template-based websites** ($2,000–$8,000) use pre-designed themes customized with your branding and content. You get basic pages, a contact form, and limited customization. These deploy in 2–4 weeks but look like templates — which matters when a homeowner is choosing who installs $30,000 worth of solar panels on their roof.

**Custom-designed websites** ($8,000–$25,000) are built from scratch around your brand and the solar buying journey. They include conversion features like solar savings calculators, financing information, project galleries, local SEO optimization, and CRM integration. Build time is typically 6–12 weeks with a traditional agency.

**Enterprise solutions** ($25,000–$100,000+) are for multi-location solar companies that need customer portals, real-time monitoring dashboards, inventory systems, and complex integrations. Unless you're running 10+ locations or managing hundreds of installs simultaneously, you don't need this tier.

The cost gap between template and custom isn't about aesthetics — it's about conversion infrastructure. A template gives you a digital brochure. A custom build gives you a lead generation system.

## What features does a solar company website actually need?

Most solar company websites are overbuilt or underbuilt. Here's what you need to turn visitors into booked installs, and what you can skip.

### Must-have features (tier 1)

**Outcome-focused homepage.** Your homepage headline should name who you serve (solar installation companies or homeowners) and the result they get. "Solar Installation in Austin — Save Up to 90% on Electricity" beats "Powering a Brighter Tomorrow." Be specific. Be local. Be clear about the outcome.

**Proof — real project photos.** Homeowners spending $20,000 to $40,000 need to see you've done this before. A gallery of real installs — rooftop panels, ground mounts, finished projects — builds more trust than any testimonial. If you're just starting and don't have client work yet, show demo builds or training projects. Real beats stock photos every time.

**Mobile-responsive design.** Over 60% of solar searches happen on mobile. Your site must load fast (under 3 seconds), have a tap-to-call button on every page, and let someone request a quote without zooming or endless scrolling. Test it on an actual phone — if you can't find the call button in two taps, you're losing leads.

**Booking system or quote form.** The easier you make it to book, the more calls you get. A direct booking link (Calendly, Acuity) removes friction. If you prefer quote forms, keep them short — name, phone, address, monthly electric bill. Every extra field costs you conversions.

**Basic local SEO setup.** Your homepage needs your city and state in the main headline, your business name and phone number must match your Google Business Profile exactly, and your site needs to load fast with clean code. This is the foundation that helps you show up when someone searches "solar installer near me."

### Worth adding once you have steady leads (tier 2)

**Solar savings calculator.** A calculator that estimates monthly savings based on a homeowner's electric bill is one of the highest-converting features in solar web design. It captures leads (name, email, phone required to see results) and pre-qualifies them based on bill size. A homeowner with a $400/month bill is a different prospect than one with a $100 bill.

Cost to add: $1,500–$5,000 depending on complexity.

**Financing information page.** Most homeowners finance solar. A clear page explaining loan options, lease terms, and incentives (federal tax credits, state rebates, net metering) answers the "how do I pay for this" question before they call. This doesn't require integration — just a well-written page.

**Service area pages.** If you serve multiple cities, each city needs its own page with local content: utility company details, average savings in that area, local permitting notes. These pages help you rank for "[city] solar installation" searches. One page per city, real local content — not just templates that swap the city name.

### Skip until you're scaling (tier 3)

**Customer portals.** Real-time monitoring dashboards and customer login areas sound impressive but add $5,000–$15,000+ to the build and require ongoing maintenance. Unless you're managing 50+ active customers, you don't need this yet.

**Live chat or chatbots.** AI-powered chat assistants cost $3,000–$8,000 to set up and $100–$300/month to run. They work for high-traffic sites but add complexity for a solar company just starting out. A phone number and a quote form work fine until you're getting 50+ leads per month.

**Advanced CRM integrations.** Connecting your website to HubSpot, Salesforce, or Jobber can cost $2,000–$10,000+ depending on the system. Start with a simple Google Sheet or a lightweight CRM (like Airtable or Notion) until the volume justifies the cost.

The pattern: start with the features that turn a visitor into a call. Add complexity only when lead volume or operational pain justifies it.

## What makes a good solar company website?

A good solar website does three things:

1. **Loads fast.** Under 3 seconds on mobile. Over 50% of visitors bounce if your site is slow. Uncompressed project photos and bloated WordPress plugins are the usual culprits.

2. **Answers the three questions every homeowner asks:**
   - What will it cost?
   - How much will I save?
   - How do I get started?

3. **Makes it easy to take the next step.** Every page has a clear call-to-action (CTA) — book a call, request a quote, calculate savings. The CTA button is visible, tappable on mobile, and uses low-stakes language like "free quote" or "30-minute call, no pressure."

Everything else — the fonts, the colors, the animations — is secondary. Speed, clarity, and a clear path to book are what turn visitors into customers.

## How to choose a web designer for your solar company

Ask these five questions before you commit:

**1. Have you built websites for solar companies before?**

Solar is different from general contracting. Homeowners research for weeks, compare multiple installers, and need specific information: financing options, federal tax credits, savings projections, and proof you're licensed and insured. A designer who understands the solar buying journey builds a better site than one who treats it like any other service business.

**2. What's the timeline from kickoff to live?**

If they say "8–12 weeks," ask what drives that. Is it design revisions? Waiting on content? Building custom features? A clear process compresses timelines. Some agencies deliver in a week. Others take three months. Know what you're signing up for.

**3. What happens after launch?**

Some agencies require ongoing retainers ($500–$2,000/month). Others hand you the keys and charge only for future updates. Neither is wrong, but know the cost before you commit. Ask: "If I want to update a page or add a new service six months from now, what does that cost?"

**4. Will I be able to update the site myself?**

Some sites are locked to the agency — you need them for every change. Others are built on platforms (WordPress, Webflow) where you can edit text and images yourself. If you want control, ask upfront.

**5. What does SEO setup include?**

"SEO included" means different things to different agencies. At minimum, you need: fast page speed, mobile-responsive design, proper heading structure, meta titles and descriptions, local business schema markup, and your city in the homepage headline. Ask what's included and what costs extra.

## Do you need a website if you're just starting a solar company?

Yes — but maybe not the $15,000 version yet.

If you're pre-revenue or getting your first few customers from door-knocking and referrals, start with a simple 3–5 page site: homepage, services, proof (even if it's training installs), process, and contact. Budget: $3,000–$6,000. Get it live, use it to close the customers you're already talking to, and upgrade once you have steady revenue.

If you're already booking installs and turning away leads, a $10,000–$15,000 custom site with conversion features (calculator, local SEO, service area pages) pays for itself in one or two jobs.

The mistake is either waiting too long (losing credibility and leads) or overbuilding too early (spending $25,000 before you know what converts). Match the site to the stage you're in.
